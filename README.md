# NYC-Vehicle-Collision-Analysis-Crash-Trends-Road-Safety-Risk-Factors
# Project Overview
This project analyzes New York City motor vehicle collision data reported by the New York City Police Department (NYPD) to identify crash trends, high-risk boroughs, major contributing factors, and patterns in injuries and fatalities.

The analysis examines collision patterns across New York City's five boroughs, changes in crash volumes over time, the most frequently reported contributing factors, and the road users most affected by collisions.

The goal was to turn collision data into meaningful insights and practical recommendations that could support road safety planning, traffic management, and injury prevention.

# Business Problem
Understanding where and why road collisions occur is important for identifying areas that may require targeted safety interventions. However, overall crash counts alone do not explain when collisions happen, which factors contribute to them, or which road users experience the most severe outcomes.

This project addressed four key questions:
Are vehicle collisions increasing or decreasing over time?
Which NYC boroughs record the highest number of collisions?
What are the leading contributing factors, and how do they vary across boroughs?
Which road users experience the highest numbers of injuries and fatalities?
Tools & Technologies
SQL: Data querying and analysis.
Microsoft Power BI: Data visualization, dashboard development, and presentation of key findings.
Analysis Process

1. Collision Trend Analysis
Examined collision volumes over time to identify significant changes and highlight the period when crashes peaked.

2. Borough-Level Analysis
Compared collision counts across the five boroughs to identify areas with the highest and lowest recorded numbers of crashes.

3. Contributing Factor Analysis
Investigated reported collision factors, including driver inattention or distraction, failure to yield the right-of-way, and following too closely.

4. Injury and Fatality Analysis
Compared the impact of collisions on motorists and pedestrians, distinguishing between injury patterns and fatal outcomes.

5. Dashboard Development
Used Power BI to present the findings visually, making it easier to explore collision patterns and communicate the implications for road safety.

# Key Findings

1. Brooklyn Recorded the Highest Number of Collisions
Brooklyn recorded approximately 507,000 collisions, the highest among the five boroughs.
Staten Island recorded the lowest number, at approximately 66,000.
Brooklyn's higher collision count highlights the importance of examining locations with concentrated crash activity. However, population-adjusted rates would be needed to compare relative risk fairly.

2. Collision Volumes Peaked Around 2018
Collision volumes reached their peak around 2018 before declining in subsequent years.
A pronounced decline occurred from 2020 onward.
The report suggests that COVID-19 and reduced mobility may have contributed to the decline.

3. Driver Behaviour Was a Major Contributing Factor
Driver inattention or distraction was the leading reported contributing factor across boroughs.
Failure to yield the right-of-way and following too closely were also prominent factors.
These findings highlight the importance of driver awareness and safer driving practices.

4. Motorists Experienced the Most Injuries, While Pedestrians Recorded the Most Fatalities
Motorists accounted for the highest number of injuries.
Pedestrians recorded the highest number of fatalities.
This distinction highlights the need for road safety measures that address both injury prevention and pedestrian protection.

These findings are drawn from the project's documented analysis.

# Recommendations
Based on the findings, I recommended the following measures:
Promote Safer Driving: Strengthen driver-awareness initiatives focused on distraction, failure to yield, and following too closely.
Prioritize High-Collision Locations: Investigate and prioritize safety improvements in areas with high collision counts, particularly in Brooklyn.
Improve Pedestrian Safety: Consider safer pedestrian crossings, clearer signage, traffic-calming measures, and targeted interventions in areas with high pedestrian fatalities.
Focus on Morning Traffic: Examine the potential for targeted enforcement and traffic-management measures during morning periods, when collision volumes were reported to be higher.
Monitor Collision Trends: Continue tracking post-2020 collision patterns to distinguish temporary changes from longer-term improvements.
Use Population-Adjusted Measures: Incorporate population-adjusted collision rates in future analysis to compare boroughs more fairly.
Project Outcome

This project demonstrates my ability to use data analytics to investigate a public safety problem, identify patterns across geographic locations and time periods, analyze contributing factors, and communicate findings through data visualization.

By combining SQL analysis with Power BI dashboard development, I translated collision data into actionable recommendations for road safety planning and decision-making.

Deliverables: Power BI dashboard, project report, collision trend analysis, contributing factor analysis, and road safety recommendations.
